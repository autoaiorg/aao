
# Realistic 3D Quality Standards

## Visual direction

The target style is naturalistic, physically plausible 3D.

Do not interpret "realistic" as stylized, cartoon, toy-like,
low-poly, voxel, or flat-shaded.

## Asset quality

- Use proportionally accurate geometry.
- Use suitable high-quality 3D assets when available and licensed.
- Use physically based materials with realistic surface variation.
- Maintain coherent world scale.
- Add realistic variation instead of obvious repetition.
- Use procedural geometry where it produces adequate fidelity.

## Rendering

- Configure color management and tone mapping appropriately.
- Use physically coherent lighting and shadows.
- Apply appropriate ambient/environment lighting.
- Avoid excessive saturation, bloom and artificial outlines.
- Use realistic camera perspective and exposure.

## Prohibited shortcuts for final assets

- Primitive shapes as substitutes for detailed final objects.
- Uniform colors where surface materials require texture.
- Random object placement without environmental rules.
- Flat lighting that destroys depth and material perception.
- Placeholder assets presented as production quality.

## Visual verification

- Render the implemented scene.
- Capture representative viewpoints.
- Inspect silhouettes, materials, lighting, scale and repetition.
- Compare against supplied visual references.
- Identify visible deficiencies.
- Iterate until acceptance criteria are met or report blockers.

## Constraints

Respect the target engine and hardware performance budget.
Never claim photorealism solely because PBR is enabled.
